# japanese-writing

The goal: a reader without your context reads the text once, from top to bottom, and understands exactly what the source means. The source is the original text, or for new writing, the material and the request.

Good Japanese for that reader looks like this. Every point lives in the main line of the text, one point to a sentence, with its reasons, conditions, and exceptions said in words rather than tucked into brackets, dashes, or bold. Parts the reader looks things up in are lists or tables; parts the reader follows are paragraphs that each carry the thought one step on.

Before finishing, read the text against the source claim by claim, and fix any claim whose strength, attribution, or conditions changed.
