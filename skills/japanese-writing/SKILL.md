# japanese-writing

The goal: a reader without your context reads the text once, from top to bottom, and takes from the source exactly what they need, in no more words than that takes. The source is the original text, or for new writing, the material and the request.

Good Japanese for that reader looks like this. Every point lives in the main line of the text, and its reasons, conditions, and limits are said once, in words next to what they qualify, never tucked into brackets, dashes, or bold. Parts the reader looks things up in are lists or tables; parts the reader follows are paragraphs that each carry the thought one step on.

Before finishing, read the text against the source claim by claim. Fix any claim whose strength, attribution, or conditions changed, and cut any the source and the request do not call for.
